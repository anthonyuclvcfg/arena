RULEBOOK / 01

The arena shows no mercy.

01 / One robot only

Choose from eight chassis shapes, including Manta, Bastion, Twin Hull and the unchanged Cross, with up to six tools, a motor, and armor within 200 points. Tools can be placed anywhere on the mounting grid and rotated through 360°. Drawn lines remain decorative.

02 / You program. It fights.

Arrange up to twelve IF → DO rules. The first matching rule runs; if none match, the robot brakes. Hazard conditions and avoidance actions ignore inactive traps. When a trap switches on or off, the robot rechecks its rules immediately. The side panels slide away at battle start. Use their panel icons to hide or restore them; the arena expand icon controls both. Viewing panels during a battle never unlocks editing. There is no manual control. Moving robots automatically reverse and turn away if they become stuck against a wall; intentional braking and wall attacks are preserved.

03 / Out means out

Crossing the boundary commits a ring-out. The eliminated robot coasts until its whole outline clears the edge, with no return or further actions. The camera stays fixed during exits; robots leaving the visible apron are clipped, never zoomed out. Each wall segment has 180 HP. Impact damage depends on mass and speed and is capped at 72 per collision. Tool damage depends on type and power. Damage accumulates until a permanent gap opens.

04 / Over the top counts too

The flipper adds height and speed with a reduced vertical impulse (15% less). Above 42 units, the wall cannot stop a robot. A robot cannot drive while airborne.

05 / The floor is an enemy

Every battle rolls new hazards: fire, grinders, spikes, Tesla pads and acid. Positions, sizes and pulse timing vary. Hazards use mirrored pairs or four-way layouts depending on the mode, with every starting zone kept clear. Crowded fields use fewer traps: 6 for five robots and 4 for six to eight. Programming detects and avoids active traps only. Inactive traps and their warning lights are ignored until they activate.

06 / Last robot standing

Two to eight robots fight solo or in teams, starting with 250 chassis HP and full energy. AI practice opponents get new names, chassis, motors, armor and tools each match, within the same 200-point budget. Human players keep their builds. Choose Free-for-all, 2 vs 2, 3 vs 3, 4 vs 4, three teams of two, or four teams of two. Teammates have the same color and do not target or damage each other with weapons. Physical contact and arena hazards still apply. The last surviving robot or team wins. At 120 seconds, free-for-all ranks by exact remaining chassis HP; teams rank by the sum of their surviving members’ HP. Only equal HP at the top is a draw. The winner is announced over the arena. The full standings appear to the right of the field, or below it on narrow screens, without a modal or dimming layer. Eliminated robots rank below survivors by survival time, then HP for simultaneous eliminations. Online matches are simulated on the server.

07 / Repair on contact

Drive over a green kit to restore up to 30% of maximum chassis HP and 25% of each surviving component. Healing is capped. Destroyed parts stay broken; full-health robots leave unneeded kits alone.

08 / Finders, keepers

Drive over an amber tool to auto-mount it in one of two bonus slots. It uses shared energy and adds mass, but costs no build points. Activate all tools also uses bonus tools. Broken bonus tools can be replaced by another pickup. Supplies refresh at 30, 60 and 90 seconds; unclaimed old supplies expire. All loot is battle-only. Collecting either a repair kit or a tool starts a shared 10-second pickup cooldown for that robot. Other robots are unaffected; unavailable or unusable items do not start the cooldown. Bonus tools are yellow; at 25% HP or less they turn red and show a warning marker.

This prototype stores your identity in this browser; it is not a verified account system. Tools, four armor panels, and both drives have separate health. Broken tools stop working; broken drives reduce mobility. Debris is visual, not a physical obstacle. Chassis HP is fixed at 250. Armor still improves panel durability. Every power level increases damage, range and cooldown. Flame damage is spread across a 1.6 / 1.8 / 2.0-second yellow stream (Light / Standard / Heavy); machine guns repeat twelve-dot bursts over 1.1 seconds while a firing rule is active. Nominal Light damage is 22 per full flame emission and 15 per full gun burst; stronger levels scale it up. Changing rules stops flames and unlaunched rounds, but already-fired bullets and rockets keep flying to impact or their full range. At the end signal, launched shots finish before the final standings are confirmed; no new rounds are fired. Tools deal different amounts of damage by target; the cards show nominal damage only. Rocket splash can damage the shooter at close range in free-for-all, but allied and self weapon damage are disabled in team modes. Components are fully restored for each new battle.
PRIVATE ROOM / ONLINE

Your robot against theirs.

Up to eight pilots join the same server. Each brings one saved robot. The host chooses the mode. Free-for-all starts with 2–8 players; teams require a full balanced roster. Seats alternate between teams in join order.

This copy is open as a local file. To use rooms, run node server.js from the project folder and open http://localhost:3000.

Teams are assigned in alternating join order. No weapon friendly fire.
